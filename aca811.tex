\section*{Detailed Lecture Notes with Illustrations}
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)\\
KPIs are quantifiable measures used to gauge performance over time against strategic goals.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Characteristics of Good
\end{itemize}

KPIs: Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Timely (SMART). They should be clearly linked to strategy, actionable, and drive the right behaviors.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Hierarchy: KPIs differ by managerial level.
  \item Strategic: For senior leaders (e.g., Market Share, ROI, Customer Lifetime Value).
  \item Operational: For middle managers (e.g., On-Time Delivery \%, Defect Rate, Employee Turnover).
  \item Tactical: For front-line supervisors (e.g., Units/Hour, Machine Downtime, Order Accuracy).
  \item Illustration: A company with a customer intimacy strategy might track KPIs like: \% revenue from new products launched in last 3 years (Internal), On-time delivery to promise (Operational), First-call resolution rate (Tactical). A cost leadership firm would prioritize different KPIs, like Cost per Unit and Capacity Utilization.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Balanced Scorecard Approach (Kaplan \& Norton)}
A strategic planning and management system that translates an organization's mission and strategy into a comprehensive set of performance measures across four balanced perspectives.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Financial: "How do we look to shareholders?" (e.g., ROCE, EVA, Revenue Growth).
  \item Customer: "How do customers see us?" (e.g., Customer Satisfaction, Market Share, Retention Rate).
  \item Internal Processes: "At what must we excel?" (e.g., Process Efficiency, Quality Metrics, Innovation Cycle Time).
  \item Learning \& Growth: "Can we continue to improve and create value?" (e.g., Employee Skills, IT System Capability, Employee Engagement).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Strategic Linkage (Cause-and-Effect): The power lies in the hypothesis linking perspectives: "If we invest in employee training (Learning), then we can improve process quality (Internal), leading to higher customer satisfaction (Customer), which finally results in increased profitability (Financial)."
  \item Illustration: A simplified scorecard for a tech firm's product division:
  \item Financial: Increase divisional EVA by $15 \%$.
  \item Customer: Achieve \#1 ranking in industry user-satisfaction survey.
  \item Internal: Reduce software bug resolution time by $30 \%$.
  \item Learning: Achieve $95 \%$ technical staff certification on new platform.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Economic Value Added (EVA) Analysis}
EVA is a measure of a company's true economic profit-the profit that exceeds the required rate of return on all capital invested.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Formula: EVA $=$ Net Operating Profit After Taxes (NOPAT) - (Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) * Total Capital Employed)
  \item NOPAT: Operating profit adjusted for taxes (often removing financing costs).
  \item Capital Employed: Total assets (or equity + interest-bearing debt), often adjusted for accounting distortions (e.g., capitalizing R\&D).
  \item WACC: The blended average cost of both debt and equity capital.
  \item Interpretation: A positive EVA means the unit/company is creating shareholder wealth. A negative EVA is destroying wealth, even if accounting profit is positive.
  \item Illustration: Division has NOPAT of $\$ 5$ million. It uses $\$ 40$ million of capital. The corporate WACC is $10 \%$.
  \item Capital Charge $=\$ 40 \mathrm{M} * 10 \%=\$ 4 \mathrm{M}$.
  \item EVA = \$5M - \$4M = \$1 Million.
  \item This division created $\$ 1$ million of economic value after covering all operating costs and the cost of its capital.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Benchmarking and Comparative Analysis}
The continuous process of measuring products, services, and practices against the toughest competitors or recognized industry leaders.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Types:
  \item Internal: Comparing performance between departments, teams, or shifts within the same organization.
  \item Competitive: Comparing key performance metrics against direct competitors (using public data, reverse engineering).
  \item Functional/Generic: Comparing similar processes (e.g., order fulfillment, logistics) to best-in-class companies outside one's industry.
  \item Process: Plan (identify what to benchmark) → Collect Data → Analyze Gaps → Adapt and Implement Improvements → Review.
  \item Illustration: A hospital's patient admission process is slow. Through functional benchmarking, it studies the check-in process of a luxury hotel chain, adapting their streamlined, technology-driven approach to reduce patient wait times by $40 \%$.
\end{itemize}
