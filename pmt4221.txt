Which of the following best defines a fuel?
A. A substance used only for heating water
B. A substance that releases heat energy during combustion
C. A material used only in engines
D. A chemical used for cooling systems
ANSWER: B

Which of the following fuels is classified as a gaseous fuel?
A. Diesel
B. Coal
C. Natural gas
D. Coke
ANSWER: C

Fuels that occur naturally in nature without processing are called:
A. Manufactured fuels
B. Synthetic fuels
C. Natural fuels
D. Artificial fuels
ANSWER: C

What is the main difference between Higher Calorific Value (HCV) and Lower Calorific Value (LCV)?
A. HCV excludes the latent heat of water vapor
B. LCV includes the latent heat of condensation
C. HCV includes latent heat of vaporization while LCV excludes it
D. Both values are always equal
ANSWER: C

Which component of proximate analysis represents the non-combustible residue remaining after complete combustion?
A. Moisture
B. Volatile matter
C. Ash
D. Fixed carbon
ANSWER: C

In proximate analysis, fixed carbon is determined by:
A. Direct measurement in the furnace
B. Subtracting moisture and volatile matter only
C. Calculating by difference from the other components
D. Measuring oxygen content
ANSWER: C

Ultimate analysis of fuel determines which of the following elements?
A. Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and sulfur
B. Moisture, ash, and volatile matter
C. Carbon dioxide and oxygen
D. Carbon and ash only
ANSWER: A

The stoichiometric air-fuel ratio refers to:
A. The maximum air used in combustion
B. The exact amount of air required for complete combustion
C. The minimum amount of air required
D. The amount of fuel burned in excess air
ANSWER: B

If the actual air-fuel ratio is greater than the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio, the mixture is called:
A. Rich mixture
B. Stoichiometric mixture
C. Lean mixture
D. Saturated mixture
ANSWER: C

Which of the following gases indicates incomplete combustion in flue gas analysis?
A. CO2
B. O2
C. CO
D. N2
ANSWER: C
