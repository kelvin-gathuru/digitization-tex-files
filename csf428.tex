\section{Information Exchange and Order Initiation}
% ============================================================

Commodities receive ratings in terms of susceptibility to e-procurement. Before deciding which products to procure through an e-procurement solution, the systems and data requirements should be identified. The following dimensions must be considered:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{System Platform:} Depending on the number of users and suppliers, a choice must be made between Intranet, Extranet, or Internet. Due to its reach and ease of access, the Internet is in most instances the platform of choice.
    \item \textbf{Systems Integration:} Current systems used for purchasing (ERP, legacy systems, EDI solutions, etc.) must be evaluated to determine the integration required should e-procurement be implemented.
    \item \textbf{Information and Data Format:} Based on integration needs, the data format requirements should be established. Where EDI solutions exist, the possibility of using XML and its impact should be evaluated.
    \item \textbf{Business Model:} Companies must choose between buy-side, sell-side, or marketplace solutions, and also between catalogue solutions versus ERP/legacy-based solutions.
    \item \textbf{Order Initiation:} Analyze whether orders are generated on demand or forecast and how they are generated (JIT, MRP, by end users, etc.). Determine if inventory is centralized or decentralized and whether inventory information is available.
\end{itemize}

From this analysis, the preferred solution is derived for each dimension to determine (1) the preferred e-procurement solution, and (2) which products to purchase using the solution.

\subsection{Commodity Feasibility and Recommended E-Procurement Solution}

Based on the results of the previous analysis, the most feasible solution for each aspect should be derived. This will indicate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The most preferred platform (Internet, Extranet, or Intranet).
    \item The integration required (with ERP, legacy systems, etc.).
    \item The preferred data format (e.g., XML/EDI).
    \item The recommended e-procurement business model (buy-side, sell-side, or marketplace).
\end{itemize}

Using the recommended solution, current e-procurement offerings should be researched to determine which best addresses the IT requirements. Should no solution adequately fit, the possibility of developing a new system should also be considered.

\section{E-Marketplace Types}
% ============================================================

There are many different types of e-marketplaces based on a range of business models. They can be broadly divided into categories based on the way in which they are operated.

\subsection{Independent E-Marketplace}
An independent e-marketplace is usually a business-to-business online platform operated by a third party, open to buyers or sellers in a particular industry. By registering, you can access classified ads or requests for quotations or bids in your industry sector. There will typically be some form of payment required to participate (e.g., eBay Market).

\subsection{Buyer-Oriented E-Marketplace}
A buyer-oriented e-marketplace is normally run by a consortium of buyers to establish an efficient purchasing environment. It helps buyers lower administrative costs and achieve the best price from suppliers. As a supplier, it provides a channel to advertise your catalogue to a pool of relevant customers.

\subsection{Supplier-Oriented E-Marketplace}
Also known as a supplier directory, this marketplace is set up and operated by a number of suppliers seeking to establish an efficient sales channel to a large number of buyers. They are usually searchable by product or service offered. Supplier directories benefit buyers by providing information about suppliers for markets and regions they may not be familiar with.

\subsection{Vertical and Horizontal E-Marketplaces}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vertical e-marketplaces} provide online access to businesses vertically up and down every segment of a particular industry sector (e.g., automotive, chemical, construction, textiles). Using a vertical e-marketplace can increase operating efficiency and help decrease supply chain costs, inventories, and procurement cycle time.
    \item \textbf{Horizontal e-marketplaces} connect buyers and sellers across different industries or regions. They can be used to purchase indirect products such as office equipment or stationery.
\end{itemize}

\section{Catalogues and E-Marketplaces}
% ============================================================

Catalogues and directories are used in many e-marketplaces, providing searchable databases of information about suppliers, products, and services.

\subsection{Catalogues}
There are various types of catalogues including:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Lists of general product information
    \item Detailed product information
    \item Information sources
    \item Promotional catalogues
\end{itemize}

Pricing information is sometimes only available to registered users. Some e-marketplaces provide a single catalogue of all suppliers and products, while others link to individual supplier catalogues. Customers can research a wide range of products and buy in one transaction from a single site.

\subsection{Directories}
Some e-marketplaces offer a directory of suppliers listed by products or services, with links to supplier websites. Being listed in the right industry sector and all relevant listings helps customers find you and improves your website's search engine rankings.

\section{Benefits of E-Marketplaces}
% ============================================================

\subsection{General Business Benefits}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Greater opportunities for suppliers and buyers to establish new trading partnerships within or across supply chains.
    \item Greater transparency in the purchasing process --- availability, prices, and stock levels are accessible in an open environment.
    \item Round-the-clock operation removes time constraints and problems with different office hours for international trade.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Benefits for the Buyer}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Updated price and availability information makes it easier to secure the best deal.
    \item Convenient way to compare prices and products from a single source.
    \item Established e-marketplaces provide a level of trust as buyers deal exclusively with vetted suppliers.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Benefits for the Seller}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Regular requests for quotations from both new and current customers.
    \item Provides an additional sales channel to market and sell products.
    \item Can offer reduced marketing costs compared with other sales channels.
    \item International e-marketplaces can provide opportunities for overseas sales.
\end{itemize}

\section{Deciding if an E-Marketplace is Appropriate}
% ============================================================

The following issues should be considered when assessing whether to participate in an e-marketplace:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Industry Fit:} Is the purpose of the e-marketplace compatible with your business strategy? Ensure you understand who buys from it and whether your business fits the profile.
    \item \textbf{Management:} Establish the ownership and financial backing of the e-marketplace. Ensure a competitor is not the major owner.
    \item \textbf{Costs:} Identify all charges --- commissions, membership fees, listing fees, and any costs associated with switching to another e-marketplace.
    \item \textbf{Marketing:} Does the e-marketplace have a strong brand? What are its marketing plans and is it likely to attract the right type of customers?
    \item \textbf{Design:} Does the overall design and functionality make it easy for purchasers to locate and buy products? Will your logo and brand image be clearly displayed?
    \item \textbf{Technical Issues:} Is the e-marketplace adequately staffed for round-the-clock service? Are there costs to make your IT systems compatible?
\end{itemize}

\section{E-Procurement and E-Sourcing}
% ============================================================

\subsection{E-Procurement}
E-procurement is the use of the internet to operate the transactional aspects of requisitioning, authorizing, ordering, receipting, and payment processes for required products or services.

E-procurement covers the following areas of the buying process:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Requisition against order
    \item Authorisation
    \item Order
    \item Receipt
    \item Payment
\end{itemize}

\subsection{E-Sourcing}
E-sourcing is the use of the internet to make decisions and form strategies regarding how and where services or products are obtained. E-sourcing covers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Knowledge specification
    \item Request for quotation / e-tender / e-auction
    \item Evaluation and negotiation
    \item Agreeing contractual terms
\end{itemize}

The use of reverse auctions and online exchanges enables procurement officers to obtain better prices by encouraging competitive bidding between suppliers.

\section{Web Portals}
% ============================================================

A portal is a web site that acts as a single or multi-source entry point for all information on a specific domain. A web portal draws together a common group of people on the basis of age, profession, location, etc., and enables activities including:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Connecting business processes within and across the supply chain.
    \item Integrating applications to support business processes.
    \item Providing access to real-time, current, and consistent information.
\end{itemize}
Popular portals include Yahoo, MSN, and Google.

\subsection{Types of Portals}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vertical Portals:} Focus on one specific industry or domain, providing tools, information, articles, research, and statistics. They are subdivided into:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Corporate Portals:} Provide personalized access to selected information of a specific company.
        \item \textit{Commerce Portals:} Support business-to-business and business-to-consumer e-commerce.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Horizontal Portals:} Focus on a wide array of interests and topics for a general audience (e.g., yahoo.com, msn.com), acting as an entry point into the internet.
    \item \textbf{Enterprise Portals:} Developed for members of an intranet or enterprise network. They provide employees with timely information, document management, on-demand applications, online training, and communication tools.
    \item \textbf{Knowledge Portals:} Increase the effectiveness of knowledge workers by providing easy access to relevant information, collaboration services, sophisticated information discovery, and a knowledge map.
    \item \textbf{Corporate Portals:} Provide personalized access to information about a particular company, serving executives, employees, suppliers, and customers with access to information, business applications, and services needed to perform their jobs.
    \item \textbf{Market Space Portals:} Support business-to-business and business-to-customer e-commerce, providing software support for transactions, rich product information, and access to discussion groups with other vendors and buyers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Challenges of E-Auctions}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Challenges to Suppliers}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Buyers Acting Opportunistically:} Suppliers frequently feel that buyers are taking advantage of the reverse auction process. Higher perceptions of opportunism reduce relationship trust and increase buyer--supplier dysfunctional conflict, reducing non-price performance.
    \item \textbf{Risk of New Entrants:} Reverse auctions open the door for new entrants who may unrealistically underbid incumbents to win business, not fully understanding the customer's requirements.
    \item \textbf{Reducing Products to a Commodity:} By focusing almost exclusively on price, the reverse auction process reduces a supplier's product to a commodity, making it impossible for suppliers to express their full capabilities. More than 50\% of suppliers surveyed after a reverse auction cited this as a major disadvantage.
\end{itemize}

\section{E-Procurement Process}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Structured vs.\ Unstructured Procurement}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Structured Procurement:} Highly automated processes for need identification, ordering, and fulfillment. Used when demand is regular and product specifications are stable, allowing long-term supplier contracts and automated reordering (e.g., tooling items, welding wires, custom replacement parts).
    \item \textbf{Unstructured Procurement:} Products not suitable for automated procedures. End users take advantage of the best deals available at the time of ordering, with very broad procurement rules (e.g., office equipment and furniture).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Impact of E-Procurement on the Procurement Process}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Reduces paperwork and redundant effort, improving productivity and lowering purchasing costs.
    \item Enables companies to locate suppliers with the best prices and quality, streamlining negotiations and contracting.
    \item Takes full advantage of enterprise buying power, enabling volume discounts and ensuring purchases are made through preferred suppliers.
    \item Streamlines and automates purchasing through critical suppliers, enabling more timely and accurate order fulfillment.
    \item \textbf{Shortened procurement cycle times:} Workflow from purchase request to payment is managed electronically, reducing errors and processing time. The Aberdeen Group estimates time savings of 70 per cent.
    \item \textbf{Reduced transaction and administration costs:} Reduction in non-value-added activities (data entry, correcting paperwork) is one of the most attractive benefits of e-procurement adoption.
    \item \textbf{Improved supply chain visibility:} Successful e-business integration enables stronger relationships with customers, distributors, retailers, and suppliers.
    \item \textbf{Reduced operating and inventory costs:} Cost reductions come from reduced inventory levels, increased supplier competition, and shorter ordering cycle times.
    \item \textbf{Enhanced decision-making:} Benefits include cost reductions, improved information, increased efficiencies, self-service approaches, and integrated supplier management.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Intended Benefits of Implementing E-Procurement}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Capturing and reducing rogue spending:} E-procurement addresses spending outside of procurement contracts by providing better spending analysis and tracking, enabling the company to consolidate spending and leverage purchase volume for better supplier deals.
    \item \textbf{Cutting out distributors to drive down prices:} E-procurement enables companies to negotiate directly with manufacturers, bypassing agents and intermediaries who typically add 10--15\% to the price. Because e-procurement does not recognize international boundaries, companies can source directly from manufacturers globally.
\end{itemize}
