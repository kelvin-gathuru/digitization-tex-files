\section*{TOPIC 5: Financial Performance Monitoring \& Reporting}
Financial performance monitoring and reporting are essential components of procurement governance. They ensure that public resources are used efficiently, expenditures align with approved budgets, and procurement decisions are transparent and accountable. In healthcare settings-where financial mismanagement can directly impact service delivery-strong monitoring and reporting systems are critical for maintaining trust, meeting regulatory requirements, and improving procurement outcomes.

\subsection*{1. Financial KPIs in Procurement}
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) are measurable metrics used to assess the effectiveness, efficiency, and compliance of procurement financial activities. They help procurement teams monitor performance trends, identify gaps, and guide improvement actions.

\subsection*{1.1 Key Financial KPIs in Healthcare Procurement}
\subsubsection*{a. Cost Savings Achieved}
Measures the reduction between the estimated (or previous year's) cost and the actual cost paid. Example: Negotiating a reduction from KES 10 M to KES $8.5 \mathrm{M}=\mathrm{KES} 1.5 \mathrm{M}$ cost savings.

\subsubsection*{b. Budget Utilization Rate}
Assesses how much of the procurement budget has been used within a specific period. Formula:\\
Budget Utilization $(\%)=($ Actual Expenditure ÷ Approved Budget $) \times 100$

\subsubsection*{c. Procurement Cycle Cost}
Evaluates the administrative cost of running the procurement process from requisition to payment. Lower cycle costs reflect efficiency.

\subsubsection*{d. Compliance Rate}
Percentage of procurement activities conducted per PPADA 2015 and organizational policies. Includes items procured through:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Competitive bidding
  \item Approved suppliers
  \item Proper documentation
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{e. Cost of Emergency Procurements}
Frequent emergency purchases often indicate weak planning and poor financial forecasting-and usually increase total costs.

\subsubsection*{f. Supplier Performance Cost Impact}
Assesses how supplier delays, quality issues, or returns affect financial performance.

\subsection*{2. Budget Variance Analysis}
Budget variance analysis compares planned expenditure to actual expenditure, helping identify overspending, underspending, and misallocation.

\subsection*{2.1 Types of Variances}
\subsubsection*{a. Positive Variance (Underspending)}
Actual spending is less than budgeted.\\
Possible causes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Good cost negotiation
  \item Delayed activities
  \item Overestimation in budgeting
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{b. Negative Variance (Overspending)}
Actual spending is higher than budgeted.\\
Possible causes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Price inflation
  \item Emergency purchases
  \item Poor forecasting
  \item Unexpected service delivery needs
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2.2 Why Variance Analysis Matters}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Prevents budget overruns
  \item Helps adjust procurement plans mid-year
  \item Supports evidence-based decision-making
  \item Improves future budgeting accuracy
  \item Strengthens accountability for department expenditures
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{2.3 Example}
Budget for laboratory reagents: KES 12,000,000\\
Actual expenditure: KES 15,500,000\\
Variance: -KES 3,500,000 (negative variance → overspending)

\subsection*{3. Monitoring Tools}
Financial monitoring tools provide structured methods for tracking procurement-related expenditures and performance. They help procurement teams identify issues early and take corrective action.

\subsection*{3.1 Common Monitoring Tools}
\subsubsection*{a. Financial Monitoring Sheets}
Track budget, expenditure, commitments, and variance in real time.

\subsubsection*{b. Procurement Tracking Dashboards}
Digital dashboards showing KPI performance such as:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Cost savings
  \item Procurement lead times
  \item Supplier delivery performance
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{c. Commitments Register}
Records all financial commitments before payment is made. Prevents overspending beyond approved funds.

\subsubsection*{d. Purchase Order (PO) Tracking Tools}
Monitor whether delivered quantities and costs match what was ordered.

\subsubsection*{e. Inventory and Stock Management Tools}
Support waste reduction and control expenditure on consumables.

\subsection*{3.2 Features of Effective Monitoring Tools}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Real-time accuracy
  \item Automatic variance calculation
  \item Clear visualization (charts, color codes)
  \item User-friendly format
  \item Integrates procurement and financial data
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4. Procurement Reporting Formats}
Financial reporting provides transparency on how procurement funds are used and helps management and regulators evaluate procurement performance.

\subsection*{4.1 Types of Procurement Financial Reports}
\subsubsection*{a. Monthly Procurement Financial Report}
Includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Expenditure vs budget analysis
  \item Variance explanations
  \item Key procurement KPIs
  \item Pending commitments
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{b. Quarterly Procurement Performance Report}
Includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Progress on procurement plans
  \item Supplier performance financial impacts
  \item Cost savings achieved
  \item Forecast for next quarter
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{c. Annual Procurement Financial Report}
Includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Summary of annual expenditure
  \item Procurement cycle efficiency
  \item Major variances and corrective actions
  \item Compliance review
  \item Recommendations
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{d. Specialized Reports}
Such as:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Emergency procurement expenditure summaries
  \item Donor-funded procurement reports
  \item Capital equipment expenditure tracking
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4.2 Principles of Good Reporting}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Accuracy
  \item Timeliness
  \item Completeness
  \item Clarity and structure
  \item Alignment with PPADA reporting requirements
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{5. Audits and Feedback}
Audits evaluate whether procurement processes comply with financial regulations, policies, and best practices. They are essential for maintaining accountability and identifying improvement areas.

\subsection*{5.1 Types of Procurement Audits}
\subsubsection*{a. Internal Audits}
Conducted by organizational audit teams to ensure compliance and strengthen controls.

\subsubsection*{b. External Audits}
Performed by government agencies, donors, or independent auditors for transparency and accountability.

\subsubsection*{c. Compliance Audits}
Check whether procurement procedures follow PPADA 2015, internal policies, and ethical standards.

\subsubsection*{d. Financial Audits}
Examine financial transactions, documentation, reporting accuracy, and fraud risks.

\subsection*{5.2 Common Audit Findings in Healthcare Procurement}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Missing documentation (delivery notes, invoices)
  \item Budget variances unexplained
  \item Payments made without proper approvals
  \item Emergency procurements not justified
  \item Supplier performance issues not documented
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{5.3 Using Audit Feedback}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Strengthen controls
  \item Improve forecasting and budgeting practices
  \item Review and update procurement policies
  \item Train procurement and finance staff
  \item Enhance transparency in reporting
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{6. Integrating Financial Monitoring and Reporting into Procurement Practice}
Effective procurement requires continuous monitoring and transparent reporting. Procurement officers must:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Track funds proactively, not reactively
  \item Document decisions and expenditures fully
  \item Use KPIs for performance evaluation
  \item Provide timely financial reports for decision-makers
  \item Engage suppliers in resolving financial performance gaps
  \item Respond to audit findings with corrective action
\end{itemize}

Strong monitoring and reporting ensure that procurement decisions remain cost-effective, transparent, and aligned with organizational and national health system goals.

\subsection*{7. Summary of Key Concepts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Financial KPIs measure procurement efficiency and accountability.
  \item Budget variance analysis helps identify problems early.
  \item Monitoring tools provide structure and visibility of expenditure.
  \item Reporting formats enhance transparency and compliance.
  \item Audits strengthen financial governance and continuous improvement.
\end{itemize}
