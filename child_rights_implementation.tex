\section*{Unit Introduction}
In this unit, we focus on ways in which we can help to implement the rights of children in our community. We will look at what citizenship means, and how communities can help enforce rights through mediation and community participation. We consider some strategies to advocate for changes to laws that do not fully protect the rights of children.

\section*{Who Is Responsible for Realising Children’s Rights?}
Of course, the answer is that we are all responsible for realising children’s rights. Let us look at how we are all responsible for children’s rights. The diagram below shows the layers of people who have an influence on children’s lives and responsibilities towards children:

These layers are similar to systems of relationships and context that affect the wellbeing of a child that we were introduced to in the onion theory in Module 1. We will use this diagram to discuss what we can do to protect children’s rights. At each level, we will also have some examples of children’s responsibilities.

\subsection*{Level 1: The Child}
The first level, at the centre of the circle, is the child. We have responsibilities to ensure that children are safe from abuse or mistreatment, have food, clothing, and are in school.

\subsection*{Level 2: The Family}
The second level of the circle is the family surrounding the child. This is the inner circle, which includes parents, siblings (brothers and sisters), and close extended family (like grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins). Parents or legal guardians have the first and most important responsibility for children. They must provide for their basic needs and protect their rights.

Where parents or guardians cannot care for a child, the CRC says that the state (government) must help. For example, if a parent is unemployed and does not have any money for food, then the government should help by providing a social grant to the family. If a child does not have a parent or guardian because they have died or abandoned the child, then the government must help by placing the child in alternative care, for example by arranging for the child to be adopted, fostered or placed in an institution (such as a children’s home) or a group care home.

\subsection*{Level 3: The Community}
The community circle includes the school and religious institutions such as a church or mosque, neighbourhood and friends. All of these structures support children and have responsibilities to ensure that children’s rights are realised.

\subsection*{Level 4: Partner Institutions}
These are NGOs, CBOs and faith-based organisations which support people and governments in realising people’s rights. In Unit 2 we learnt about ways in which NGOs support children and communities and how they must include an understanding of rights in the way in which they work. Partner institutions also include the media (newspapers, TV, radio) which help to educate people about human rights and to spread information about human rights. The media can help people know more about their rights; tell people what to do to get rights; and create a place for people to talk about rights. The media can also report stories of human rights abuse.

\section*{Community Participation}
Let us look at how we can encourage more people to become involved in making sure that children’s rights are met. First, we will look at the idea of ‘citizenship’.

\textbf{Citizenship} involves being a committed member of the community and participating in community issues and activities to improve the well-being of all. It includes being politically active, voting, and helping to solve problems that affect people.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Citizenship}: The duties and responsibilities that people have in their country.
    \item \textbf{Civic Participation}: Community service, attending public meetings, fundraising, or starting task forces.
    \item \textbf{Fundraising Activities}: Events like concerts and plays where the money made is used for a special project.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Participation}
The concept of \textbf{Ubuntu} (‘Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu’) means ‘You are because others are’. It is at the heart of citizenship and civic participation. Meeting the needs of children at risk calls for the combined work of all members of society.

\section*{Advocacy}
\textbf{Advocacy} means working for change. it involves actions taken by groups or individuals to persuade those responsible to change policies and practices.

\subsection*{Advocacy Examples}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Abubacer’s Story (Mozambique)]
During the war in Mozambique, thousands of children were displaced or forced to be soldiers. Abubacer decided to act, helping children trace their parents and providing basic needs like food and medicine, despite the danger.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Freedom’s Story (Ivory Coast)]
Freedom fought for democracy and freedom of the press in Ivory Coast. After exposing government corruption, he was imprisoned in Maca Prison but continued to fight for a better future for his children.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[title=Catherine’s Story (Malawi)]
After learning she was HIV-positive, Catherine Phiri went public and founded SASO to help others. Her work grew to support thousands of orphans and households, eventually gaining UN recognition.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section*{Advocacy Action}
Good advocacy involves:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Understanding the Issues}: Learning as much as possible and finding allies.
    \item \textbf{Building Interest}: Using posters, plays, debates, and the media.
    \item \textbf{Setting Goals}: Identifying clear objectives and whom to influence.
    \item \textbf{Getting Organised}: Creating an action plan.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Escalation Model of Advocacy Action}
We recommend a gradual approach:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Direct Engagement}: Compliments, assertive feedback, and clear requests.
    \item \textbf{Report to Seniors}: Escalating to higher management or national departments.
    \item \textbf{External Confrontation}: Legal action, media attention, or mass action.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Unit Summary}
We are all responsible for realising children's rights across five levels: the child, family, community, partner institutions, and government. Through citizenship, civic participation, and advocacy, we can build a culture of respect for human rights and create lasting change.
