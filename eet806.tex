\section{Introduction to Neural Networks}
% ============================================================

Neural networks are techniques used to perform different tasks and make decisions. They consist of processing elements connected together in parallel and series combinations. In biological systems, they are cells connected together loosely as nodes that facilitate the transmission of signals from one location to another.

% ============================================================
\section{Artificial Neural Networks}
% ============================================================

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are mathematical techniques used to perform different tasks and make different decisions. They are processing elements connected together imitating the human brain structure, arranged as nodes in series and parallel combinations. ANNs are a branch of \textbf{Artificial Intelligence}.

\subsection{Applications of Artificial Neural Networks}

Artificial neural networks are widely applied across many fields, including:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Transport:} High-speed train control systems.
    \item \textbf{Communication:} Mobile phones, communication networks, and load balancing.
    \item \textbf{Data Mining:} Pattern discovery in large datasets.
    \item \textbf{Entertainment:} Video games.
    \item \textbf{Medicine:} Surgical assistance, medical image processing, pattern recognition, and artificial limbs.
    \item \textbf{Military:} Drone control systems, missile guidance systems, and autonomous vehicles and pilots.
    \item \textbf{Instrumentation:} Artificial musical instruments such as electronic keyboards, pianos, and guitars.
    \item \textbf{Astronomy:} Study and analysis of celestial bodies.
    \item \textbf{Biometrics:} Voice recognition, fingerprint recognition, face recognition, and human feature identification.
    \item \textbf{Training and Research:} E-learning systems and humanoid robots.
    \item \textbf{Scientific Research:} Pattern recognition to improve existing systems and process modelling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Historical Perspective of Neural Networks}
% ============================================================

The human brain is considered the most intelligent biological system on earth. Scientists attempted to develop systems capable of imitating the operations of the human brain, an effort that led to the development of computers in the 1940s.

Early computers were capable of executing many instructions per second and were faster than human beings at performing arithmetic operations. One of the early architectures was the \textbf{Von Neumann Model}, where the processing unit was separated from the memory unit. Although these computers could execute large numbers of instructions quickly, they lacked the ability to learn from experience.

This limitation motivated researchers to develop systems that could learn from data, ultimately leading to the development of neural networks --- a concept that imitates the operation of neurons in the human brain.

Key milestones in the development of neural networks include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{1873 -- 1890:} Early concepts related to neural processing were proposed by Alexander Bain (1873) and William James (1890).
    \item \textbf{1943:} McCulloch and Pitts introduced a mathematical model of a neuron, forming the foundation of modern neural networks. Their concept integrated processing and memory in a structure similar to biological neurons.
    \item \textbf{1956:} The term \textit{Artificial Intelligence} was coined at the Dartmouth Conference. Artificial intelligence contains several branches, one of which is artificial neural networks.
    \item \textbf{1958:} Frank Rosenblatt developed the \textbf{Perceptron}, a learning algorithm that formed the basis for Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs). The perceptron could perform simple operations such as addition and subtraction.
    \item \textbf{1969:} Minsky and Papert demonstrated that the single-layer perceptron could not solve the XOR problem. This criticism led to the \textit{Neural Network Winter}, during which funding for neural network research declined significantly.
    \item \textbf{1975:} The \textbf{Backpropagation} learning algorithm was developed, enabling neural networks to solve more complex problems such as classification and medical diagnosis.
    \item \textbf{1980s:} The concept of \textit{connectionism} emerged, emphasizing parallel processing using interconnected processing elements.
    \item \textbf{1990s:} Neural networks were integrated with fuzzy logic systems to form \textbf{Neuro-Fuzzy Systems}, particularly the Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS). These hybrid systems are capable of solving complex problems using advanced learning and training algorithms.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Methods for Neural Networks}

Several training methods have been developed for neural networks, including:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Gradient Descent
    \item Genetic Algorithms
    \item Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)
    \item Ant Colony Optimization
\end{itemize}

\section{Biological Inspiration}
% ============================================================

Artificial neural networks are inspired by the structure and functioning of the human brain, which contains billions of interconnected neurons.

\subsection{The Human Brain}

The human brain is the central organ responsible for processing information and controlling body functions. It contains approximately 100 billion neurons connected together to perform various tasks. The brain has two hemispheres:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Left Hemisphere:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Responsible for logical reasoning and rule-based operations.
        \item Handles arithmetic and logical calculations.
        \item Processes structured information.
        \item Neurons in the left hemisphere are generally associated with sequential processing and rule-based systems, similar to expert systems used in computing.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Right Hemisphere:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Responsible for image processing, pattern recognition, and control functions.
        \item Handles visual information and spatial processing.
        \item Neurons in the right hemisphere operate in highly parallel structures, which inspired the design of artificial neural networks.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
